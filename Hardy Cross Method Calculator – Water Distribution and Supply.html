What the Hardy Cross method does

Hardy Cross is an iterative method for finding pipe flows in a water network so that the head loss around every closed loop adds up to zero. It starts from a guessed flow distribution that satisfies continuity at each junction, then corrects the flows loop by loop.

Flows that run clockwise around the loop are taken as positive. Head loss in a pipe carries the same sign as its flow, so a balanced loop has its clockwise and counter-clockwise losses cancelling out.

Hazen–Williams

The Hazen–Williams equation estimates friction loss in pressurised water pipes. The coefficient C describes pipe roughness: a smoother pipe has a higher C and loses less head.

Scope of this version

This version handles one closed loop of 3 to 10 pipes. Each junction's implied outflow is taken from your initial flows and stays fixed during the iterations. Networks with several interconnected loops, pumps and valves are not modelled yet.

Head loss (Hazen–Williams, SI units)

hf = 10.67 × L × |Q|^1.852 / (C^1.852 × D^4.871)

L is pipe length (m), Q is flow (m³/s), C is the Hazen–Williams coefficient and D is diameter (m). The sign of hf follows the sign of Q.

Flow correction

ΔQ = − Σhf / [1.852 × Σ(|hf / Q|)]

The same ΔQ is added to every pipe flow in the loop. Since |hf/Q| = r × |Q|^0.852, where r = 10.67 L / (C^1.852 × D^4.871), the calculator uses that form.

Updated discharge

Q_new = Q_old + ΔQ

Iteration stops when |ΔQ| is smaller than the tolerance you set on the Calculator page.

Step-by-step procedure

  1. Number the pipes in order around the loop, clockwise, and give each one a name.
  2. Enter the length, diameter and Hazen–Williams C for each pipe.
  3. Enter an initial flow for each pipe. Use positive values for clockwise flow and negative values for counter-clockwise flow. No flow may be zero.
  4. For each pipe, compute r = 10.67 L / (C^1.852 D^4.871), then hf = r × Q × |Q|^0.852.
  5. Sum the signed head losses (Σhf) and sum r × |Q|^0.852 (which equals Σ|hf/Q|).
  6. Compute ΔQ = −Σhf / (1.852 × Σ|hf/Q|) and add it to every pipe flow.
  7. Repeat until |ΔQ| is below the tolerance. The final flows are the balanced discharges.

Sample loop

The sample is a four-pipe loop named P1 to P4. Its initial flows are chosen so that the implied junction outflows balance, which is the condition the method needs. Click the button to load it into the calculator and run it.

Once it runs, the results show each pipe's initial and corrected flow, the head loss per pipe, the direction of flow and every iteration. The success banner confirms when the loop has converged within your tolerance.

Reading the results

A balanced loop has a total signed head loss close to zero. A positive corrected flow runs clockwise around the loop and a negative one runs counter-clockwise.
